J3 · multi-start MAP-CEM · 200 candidates · + 12-clip confirmation

The optimizer worked. The objective it optimised does not hold up.

CEM converged, and independent restarts agreed — but it recovered a rubber duck deader than a brass pot. Chasing that down retracts the J2 pass: the judge’s restitution preference for the duck reverses when friction alone changes, and the effect that was measured was never bigger than the noise.

retraction — J2 kill test

J2 was reported as a qualified PASS on the strength of a cross-object sign flip: ρ(score, e) = +0.800 for the rubber duck and −0.700 for the brass pot, gap +1.500, permutation p = 0.013. The duck half does not reproduce. Re-measured in the same engine with the same judge and the same prompts, changing only friction from μ = 0.9 to μ ≈ 0.4, the duck’s ranking over the same four damping values inverts completely (ρ = −1.000).

Without a stable duck, there is no sign flip, and the pass criterion is not met. The brass pot’s preference for dead motion does reproduce and stands. J2 should be read as: one object out of four showed a reproducible material preference.

But the duck lands at e ≈ 0.04 and the pot at e ≈ 0.17: the toy rubber duck is recovered as the less bouncy of the two. Multi-start agreement is a property of the search, not evidence that the recovered physics is right — which is exactly why the confirmation below exists.

Confirmation: the renderer was not the culprit

The search ran on EEVEE for speed. My first hypothesis was that CEM had exploited a proxy artefact. It had not. The same θ rendered in both engines agree well — and Cycles, the engine J2 validated, also prefers the deader duck at this friction.

Where this leaves J3

  • The harness is built and works. Sim → render → judge → CEM runs end to end at ~19 s per candidate, 200/200 valid, restarts converging. Nothing here needs rebuilding.
  • Absolute per-clip scores are not a usable objective. SEM 0.29–0.80 against spans of 0.56–1.60, and a nuisance parameter that flips the ranking. Averaging three paraphrases does not fix this; halving the SEM needs roughly four times the calls.
  • Re-run the J2 kill test at two or more friction slices before trusting any material claim. Validating at a single arbitrary μ is what let this through — μ = 0.9 was chosen to keep objects on the table, not for physical reasons.
  • Try pairwise A-vs-B scoring. Per-clip offsets and prompt framing cancel in “which of these two moves more like a rubber duck?”, which is the noise term that dominates here. Cheap to test against the clips already rendered.
