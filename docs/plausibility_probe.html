Cosmos 3 cookbook prompt · A/B logits · authored violations

It scores an object that doubles in size as more plausible than real physics

The cookbook plausibility question is a far better instrument than anything used before — 20× the dynamic range and a ranking that survives rewording. But given clips that violate the exact principles it names, it does not detect them. It ranks by how much the clip moves.

The prompts, verbatim

All scored the same way: forced single-token answer, score = logprob(A) − logprob(B) read from the logits at the first answer position. No sampling, no chain of thought. The final projection runs in fp32 because the model’s bf16 head quantises the margin to multiples of 0.125.

1 — plausibility (Cosmos 3 cookbook form)

Is this video physically plausible/possible according to your understanding of e.g. object permanence, shape constancy (objects maintain shape over time), continuous trajectories of objects? Assume it is the normal laws of physics.
Your answer should be based on the events in the video and ignore the quality of the simulation engine.
(A) Possible
(B) Impossible

2 — naturalness

How natural does the motion in this video look, compared to how this object would move in the real world? Assume the normal laws of physics.
Your answer should be based on the events in the video and ignore the quality of the simulation engine.
(A) Natural
(B) Unnatural

3 — material consistency

Is the way this object moves consistent with what it is made of? Consider its weight, hardness and how much such a material should bounce. Assume the normal laws of physics.
Your answer should be based on the events in the video and ignore the quality of the simulation engine.
(A) Consistent
(B) Inconsistent

Swelling to 2.2× and teleporting sideways both add pixel motion, and both score higher than the valid clip. The only violation scored lower is the vanishing object — which also removes motion, so even that is the confound rather than a detection. My control design is at fault there and it should not be counted as evidence either way.

What is worth keeping from this

  • The prompt is a much better instrument. Dynamic range 6.2 points against ~0.3 for pairwise margins, and three differently-worded variants rank the clips within 0.06 of each other — where previously paraphrase spread exceeded the signal entirely.
  • Naming the principles did not make them checkable. The prompt explicitly lists object permanence, shape constancy and continuous trajectories, and the model detects violations of none of them.
  • “Ignore the simulation quality” was the right instruction and removes a confound I spent a lot of render work chasing — but it does not create a physics prior that is not there.
  • The confound is now measured four independent ways: ρ(score, magnitude) = −0.94 on valid clips, +0.85 on pairwise margins, +1.000 across these conditions, and bounce counts of 1–2 against a true 1–21.
