The parameters move. The steps that moved them are coin flips.

Every object starts at μ = 0.40 and ends somewhere else, which looks like a fit until you read the dy column: the sign of each SPSA step flips about half the time. A converging fit has consistently signed steps. This is a random walk on a flat objective, and the endpoints are where the walk happened to stop.

Per-iteration trajectory

Each SPSA step evaluates the SAME object at μ− and μ+, one perturbation either side. dy = y(μ+) − y(μ−) is the entire signal driving the update — and because both clips are the same object, any per-object bias in the judge cancels by construction.

what the dy column says

Sign consistency: book 3/8, brass pot 4/8, wooden bowl 5/8. Indistinguishable from a coin. Median |dy| is 0.21–0.35, the same size as the step-to-step scatter, so the update is being driven by noise rather than by any dependence of the score on friction.

How the simulation looks as μ changes

Same ramping tilt (4° → 34°), rendered at the parameters the fit held at three points, cropped per object. They look nearly identical — and that is the informative part, not a rendering fault: over the range the fit explored, friction changes the slip onset by a few degrees, which is a handful of frames. If the difference is hard for you to see, it was hard for the judge too.
