01What the pipeline recovers

The wooden bowl's friction is the first value to land in the textbook range for wood on wood (0.3–0.6) rather than an order of magnitude below it. That is not luck: the bowl is flat-bottomed and genuinely slides, whereas the spheres roll, and a rolling object's deceleration measures rolling resistance rather than the Coulomb μ a simulator consumes. Probe validity depends on object shape, and the bowl is the first object for which the slide probe is the right experiment at all.

The rubber duck does not: measured 0.125, but the simulator cannot get below 0.194.

No contact damping across a range spanning four orders of magnitude reproduces the measured rebound for that mesh. The recovered parameter is not physically realisable in our simulator — a genuine negative result rather than a fitting failure, and exactly the check that treating the simulator as a hard constraint exists to perform.

04The estimator was manufacturing numbers

The retired fitter searched a parameter grid for the value whose hand-weighted motion signature best matched the track. A grid always returns a best match; it never asks whether the observation constrains anything. This comparison runs both estimators over identical tracks.

05The audit that corrected itself

An earlier version of this report stated that 68 of 93 takes contained no measurable motion. That came from tracked centroids, and the tracker fails on these clips in a way its own diagnostics cannot see: points stay “visible” while sitting on the background, so a duck that visibly fell to the table measured 6 px of motion. Re-run with appearance matching, 71 of 93 takes move and only 8 are genuinely static.

The brass pot stops being a brass pot.

All 14 degraded takes are the same object, which transforms mid-clip from a lidded pot into a wide shallow bowl. No density, friction or restitution turns a pot into a bowl, so this fails even a plausibility bar. It is asset integrity, not identifiability.

06Corrections

  • “The videos do not contain the experiment” was an instrument artefact, stated twice on tracker evidence. 71 of 93 takes move.
  • The published baseball restitution was 0.000 — no bounce at all. On validated tracks it is 0.215 ± 0.060; the point tracker was missing the rebound outright.
  • The noise floor was assumed, and assumed too low (1.50 px against a measured 2.04 px), so every earlier interval was about a third too narrow.
  • A claimed ~80% yield for drop and slide was wrong; the real drop yield is 29%. “Moves” and “yields a usable observable” are different questions.
  • A max-excursion statistic is not a displacement. Used twice — first as path length, then again as peak displacement — and wrong both times.
  • 60 fps was not the diagnosis for mass and is no longer proposed as a requirement.
  • There is no ground truth in this pipeline. Errors were once quoted against “true” densities from a hardcoded table of textbook guesses.

07Known limits

  • The rubber duck's values come from clips in which it re-forms rather than translating; appearance matching cannot fully separate “translated” from “re-rendered nearby”.
  • A prediction I could not test: I expected the baseball's backwards de/dv to flip with more data. Its drop clips were already complete in the partial run, so the fit is over identical takes and the prediction was never actually put to the test.
  • The simulated panes are composites, not renders — simulated positions drawn with real pixels, anchored to the observed starting point.
  • Collide was dropped from the expanded lab at ~24% yield, so mass ratio is not measured here at all.
