The judge has no arrow of time

The plan was to recover physics parameters by having a video model say which simulated motion looks better, then optimise against it with gradients so it scales. It doesn’t work, and after fixing eight defects on our side the reason is now measured rather than guessed: on real filmed video, Cosmos3-Nano cannot tell a bouncing ball from a ball spontaneously leaping off the ground.

The decisive test, and you can watch it

Time reversal is the cleanest possible physics question: a reversed clip has identical motion magnitude to machine precision and is physically impossible. This is genuinely filmed 240 fps footage of a real bouncing ball (Wikimedia), cut to the same 12-frame format as our clips. Left is real, right is the same clip backwards. The judge was asked which shows more physically realistic motion.

8 of 14 forward, mean margin +0.007, p ~ 0.92. A coin flip. On our own renders it was significantly wrong — 3 of 16, p ~ 0.008 — so our rendering actively misleads it, but that is not the cause. Even perfect footage yields nothing to optimise against.

Why this kills the objective, not just the gate

Once frames actually reached the model, its preference resolved into one thing:

rho(margin, delta motion-magnitude)
   4 frames (what the model was really getting) : -0.008  +0.394  +0.229  -0.270
  12 frames (after the fix)                     : +0.826  +0.878  +0.816  +0.481

Given three times more frames the judge does not read more physics — it reads more motion. Partial out magnitude and friction and nothing remains. So the scalar an optimiser would ascend is ~85% motion magnitude with no arrow of time. Gradient or CEM, it inflates motion and reports it as recovered physics — which is exactly what the J3 CEM did when it returned a rubber duck deader than a brass pot.

The harness is not the problem. Every link of the chain exists and is verified, including the two added this session: a differentiable rebuild of the judge’s video preprocessing (matching the real one to 5.9e-08, which is what makes a differentiable renderer unnecessary — the sim and render already share a camera, so points→pixels is an analytic projection), and SPSA, which estimates a gradient in two evaluations regardless of parameter count where CEM needs a population that grows with dimension.

What I would do next

  • Run this exact reversal test on a larger Cosmos 3. 14 real clips, ~10 minutes, pass/fail on a question with a known answer. It decides whether the judge direction survives at all, and it costs nothing to find out.
  • Do not build G1/G2/G3 until something passes it. The gradient machinery is ready; pointing it at this objective would produce confident, wrong answers.
  • Keep the reversal test as a permanent gate. It needs no ground truth, is magnitude-matched by construction, and any judge worth optimising against must pass it.
