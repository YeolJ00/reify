Simulated videos on the real assets

Real textured objects dropped onto the wooden table in the staged scene, simulated in Newton/Warp and rendered in Cycles — the same scene, camera, HDRI and settings as the generated clips, so only the object’s motion differs between candidates. This is the candidate-generation half of the pipeline: what θ does to the motion, and what it looks like.

The scene

Wooden table, city HDRI, fixed camera. The subject drops at one central spot; the other staged objects stay put as scene context. Every candidate for a given object is pixel-identical except for how the object moves.

Pipeline, asset to clip

1real assettextured glTF from the staged scene; Y-up → Z-up fixed, decimated to 400 faces, scaled to its real size in cm
2collision geometrysphere cover built from the rotated mesh at 2 cm pitch, so contact matches the shape being rendered
3rolloutNewton/Warp rigid body, Hunt–Crossley contact max(k·pen − cd·pen·v, 0), 60 substeps/frame, dropped 0.30 m onto the tabletop
4measurerestitution from the centroid height — peak rebound speed over peak approach speed; also horizontal travel and the event window
5pose exportsim centroid → Blender origin: loc = centroid − R·v̄, the conversion that had the tracker off-object for most of the earlier project
6renderBlender 4.2 Cycles, 48 samples, the same wooden table, city HDRI, camera and settings as the generated clips — only the object’s motion differs between candidates

What θ actually does

Two swept parameters: contact damping cd, which sets restitution, and friction μ. Restitution is measured from each rollout — peak rebound speed over peak approach speed on the centroid height — not read off the knob, so the axis is physical. Cells are the measured e; darker is bouncier.

Damping dominates and friction barely touches restitution — which is the point: it gives a near-orthogonal second axis to sweep, and it is the axis that exposed the judge’s instability. Impact speed is matched at ~1.7 m/s across every cell, so nothing varies but the contact response.

What the reasoner actually says about these clips

Every judge call in this project forced a single token and read its logit. The model was never once asked, in words, what it sees. It is, and the answer splits cleanly in two.

Object and material — correct

duck, e=0.03“a rubber duck, and it is made of rubber”

So the renders do convey material identity. The Cycles work was not wasted, and rule 2 is satisfied.

True counts span 1–21 and correlate +0.818 with restitution — the signal is unambiguously in the video. The model’s answers span 1–2. At 12 frames ρ(model, true) = −0.231; given every frame it rises only to +0.301. Restitution is the parameter the whole pipeline exists to recover, and its direct observable is not resolved.

What was measured on these clips

  • Restitution spans 0.00–0.81 across the sweeps, evenly, at matched impact speed — so the candidate set genuinely covers the parameter range rather than clustering.
  • Horizontal travel rises with e (ρ ≈ +0.7). Bouncier candidates also slide further, and a handful leave frame. This is logged per clip and partialled out of every fit, because it is the obvious way a judge could look right for the wrong reason.
  • Density is held fixed at 600 for all objects. Per-object densities are not known and inventing them was a documented failure earlier in this project, so it stays a constant rather than a fabricated input.
  • Placement, geometry and drop height are inputs, not fitted. Only the contact parameters vary, which keeps θ in the smooth regime.
