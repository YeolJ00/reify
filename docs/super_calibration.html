The bigger reasoner sees physics. It still doesn’t see restitution.

Super detects authored physics violations 9/9 where Nano managed 1/9, and the motion-magnitude confound that has dogged every version of this experiment collapses from ρ = +1.000 to +0.200. But asked to rank valid rollouts by how much they bounce, it is still reading motion — so the calibration fails and no fit was run.

Why the test may have been mis-shaped

the sweep was in the superball band

Ground-truth bounce counts for that grid: 1 at e=0.04, 14 at e=0.42, 19 at e=0.60, 18 at e=0.74. A rubber duck does not bounce 18 times. Across that entire range “less bouncy is more plausible” is arguably the correct judgement for both objects — so no sign flip could appear, and a monotone statistic could only ever find motion magnitude.

The observed argmax agrees: the duck peaks at e = 0.035–0.061 (rank 1 of 10 and of 16) and the pot at 0.047–0.230. Both peaks sit at or below the bottom of the grid.

What the peak structure means for the method

there is no likelihood — it is priors all the way down

If plausibility peaks at a material-appropriate restitution rather than rising with it, then what the judge supplies is log p(θ | material) — a learned prior over parameters — not a likelihood. And that is the honest shape of this problem: the asset never physically existed, there is no footage of it, so there is nothing to condition on. The simulator restricts that prior to the physical manifold, and MAP means find the mode of the judge’s implied prior, restricted to what Newton can produce.

Two things follow. Identifiability is weak by construction — you are locating a prior mode, so any direction the prior is flat in stays unconstrained, and multi-start scatter is the honest uncertainty report rather than a diagnostic to fix. And β·s(θ) + log p(θ) was double-counting: a hand-written prior stacked on a learned one. A peaked score is also what makes the mode well defined at all — a monotone one puts it on the boundary, which is exactly where the J3 CEM ended up when it returned a rubber duck deader than a brass pot.

Caveats I would hold against this

  • This is the third statistic on the same question. Each change had a stated structural reason — monotone → peaked is a wrong-shape fix, not a loosened threshold, and it was committed before the new sweep existed. But the count itself is a warning sign, and a fourth would not be the answer.
  • int8 carries an asterisk only for negatives. Quantisation can damage a discrimination, not manufacture one, so the 9/9 violation result is safe; a marginal calibration failure would deserve a bf16 re-run across two cards.
  • n = 14 per cell. The bootstrap CI on the peak difference will be wide, and a marginal pass would be weak evidence rather than a green light.
  • Detecting a teleporting object and resolving e=0.1 from e=0.3 are different asks. Only the first is established, on both models.
