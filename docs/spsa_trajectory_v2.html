The parameters move. The steps that moved them are coin flips.

Every object starts at μ = 0.40 and ends somewhere else, which looks like a fit until you read the dy column: the sign of each SPSA step flips about half the time. A converging fit has consistently signed steps. This is a random walk on a flat objective, and the endpoints are where the walk happened to stop.

Clips, re-cut with scene context

Crops now floor at 60% of the frame in each dimension, so the table edge and the incline stay in view. Previously a barely-moving object collapsed to 74×74 — 2% of the frame — which cropped away the very tilt the motion is judged against.

The book is kept, not dropped. Its base is 6.8 cm against 10 cm tall, so it topples rather than slides — which makes it a centre-of-mass reading on the same render, not a broken friction one. Different observable, same clip.

what the dy column says

Sign consistency: book 3/8, brass pot 4/8, wooden bowl 5/8. Indistinguishable from a coin. Median |dy| is 0.21–0.35, the same size as the step-to-step scatter, so the update is driven by noise rather than by any dependence of the score on friction. Worth noting the fit ran on the OLD tight crops, where the incline was cropped out — the judge was asked about sliding without being shown the slope.
